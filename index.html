Research

I am a graduate student in Nikos Sidiropoulos's group at UVA. My PhD work is on interference-resilient communication: how a receiver can excise interference with little loss in data rate, and how it can find the timing it needs to do so quickly.

High Rate Canonical Correlation Analysis (HRCCA)

A canonical-correlation approach to recovering a signal in the presence of strong interference, with solvers, carrier frequency offset estimation and a full OFDM pipeline implemented in Rust with Python bindings.

Linearly Precoded Signal Alignment (SIGAL)

Precoding that aligns the desired signal so interference can be removed while giving up very little rate, validated with Monte Carlo simulation and over-the-air software-defined radio experiments. A companion timing-acquisition method uses a null-slot spatial proxy, with a machine-checked identifiability proof in Lean 4.

N. D. Sidiropoulos and K. Lynch, “Linearly Precoded Signal Alignment: How to Excise Interference with Little Rate Loss,” IEEE SPAWC 2025.
